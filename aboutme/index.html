calm down, oscar.

I'm Peter. Oscar is my son — he's the reason this studio has a name, and he's still working on the calm part.

My mind runs on a hundred ideas a week, though only a select few ever reach the App Store. The ones that survive are built out of real necessity: tools born to solve my own development friction, or engineered to balance a full-time career while raising my son.

I thrive on complex technical challenges and the work of piecing fragmented systems together. My background spans enterprise platforms like SAP and Microsoft Foundry, and lately, wiring LLMs into relays, on-device models, and structured data so they behave predictably. I don't fit into a single-stack box — I work as a systems architect, figuring out how disparate pieces connect and where the failure points are.

If there's a north star behind all of it, it's the same one most people building with AI right now are quietly chasing: a local, personally-hosted assistant that actually knows my context — the Jarvis thing, minus the flying suit. Unli Rice's shared memory is one piece of that; so is knowing when a task doesn't need a model at all, the way ClearSpace's photo review runs on-device signal processing instead of a VLM call.

When I'm not shipping software, you'll find me dreaming of riding motorcycles on quiet roads
